Pressure Distillation (Prior Art)

Pressure distillation relies on a hydrophobic modificaton at pore entrances to sustain water-vapor transport across the air gap; premature liquid entry collapses performance.

Technical Summary

I joined to convert an early-stage sol-gel coating concept into a physics-grounded, manufacturable membrane architecture. Work focused on thickness control, substrate/solvent compatibility, limiting pore wicking via backfilling, and diagnosing pressure-driven failure.

A multilayer approach using a compliant underlayer was used to prevent local defects from causing global breakthrough, enabling order-of-magnitude improvements in pressure stability and measurable salt rejection, while defining clear next-step failure modes.

Note: This summary intentionally avoids confidential formulations, operating conditions, and internal targets.

Overview

Osmopure is developing membranes for pressure-driven purification where a thin hydrophobic top layer must sustain high liquid entry pressures without sacrificing transport. My contribution focused on establishing quantitative coating control, resolving substrate/process failure modes, and developing a multilayer architecture that significantly increased pressure stability and enabled measurable salt rejection.
