Overview

Osmopure is developing membranes for pressure-driven purification where a thin hydrophobic top layer must sustain high liquid entry pressures without sacrificing transport. My contribution focused on establishing quantitative coating control, resolving substrate/process failure modes, and developing a multilayer architecture that significantly increased pressure stability and enabled measurable salt rejection.

Note: This case study is intentionally non-proprietary and focuses on approach, physical reasoning, and validation strategy.

Key Contributions

  • Built thickness metrology + coating calibration to connect film thickness to process conditions.
  • Resolved substrate/solvent incompatibility and decoupled coating from gelation via controlled post-cure.
  • Introduced pore backfilling to limit wicking and keep the functional layer surface-localized.
  • Diagnosed heterogeneity-driven global failure under pressure and validated a multilayer architecture to arrest breakthrough.
  • Mapped short-term/long-term failure modes and defined a roadmap for modulus/thickness optimization and cycling tests.
