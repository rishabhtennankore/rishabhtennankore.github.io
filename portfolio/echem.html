Abstract

Biological muscle achieves energy-efficient motion through distributed actuation, where low-voltage electrical signals gate access to stored chemical energy across many active units. Electrochemical actuators offer a compelling analogue, but existing systems are often constrained by low efficiency, slow response, or parasitic electrolysis. Here, we explore osmotic actuation driven by ion concentration gradients that naturally arise during Faradaic cycling across ion-selective membranes. We report a membrane architecture that combines high water permeability with cation selectivity by depositing an ultrathin (~100 nm) anionic hydrogel skin onto a microporous support, introducing Donnan exclusion with minimal mass-transfer resistance. Using this platform, we demonstrate low-voltage osmotic pressure modulation using a ferri/ferrocyanide redox couple. This work outlines a pathway toward low-voltage, distributed osmotic actuators with potential integration into redox-based energy storage systems.
