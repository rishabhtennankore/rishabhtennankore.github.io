Abstract

Fluid-based (hydraulic or pneumatic) actuators are ubiquitous in the field of soft robotics because of their high force output and large strokes, but they are fundamentally limited by the need for external pumps that make them bulky and ill-suited for applications where remote actuation is required. Turgor driven deformation and stiffness modulation represent emerging bioinspired fluidic actuation strategies that can open the door to untethered soft fluidic actuators that show unique promise for use as photomechanical devices, because of the possibility of using optically thick samples without sacrificing photochemical conversion. Here, we show a reversible, photo-responsive osmotic pressure modulation of an aqueous solution of azobenzene-containing polymer, based on the difference in polarity between the trans- and cis- isomers. Building off this result, we developed a forward-osmosis based bulging-disk actuator that shows a non-linear deformation under load, and a work capacity of 0.3 kJ/m3 at room temperature. Finally, based on the remarkable agreement between model predictions and experimental observations, we discuss the work performance of the actuator in relation to thermodynamic first principles and material properties.
