Abstract

Liquid Metal actuators offer a compelling path toward compact, efficient artificial muscles by exploiting surface tension–dominated mechanics at small length scales. Here, we present a low-voltage (<1 V) actuation strategy based on electrochemically controlled modulation of interfacial tension in eutectic gallium–indium (EGaIn). Reversible oxidation of an oxide skin enables controllable liquid metal imbibition into microscale channels, governed by favorable Laplace pressure scaling. Using experimentally validated scaling laws, we design an elastically coupled blister actuator that achieves a seven-fold increase in force per droplet relative to prior liquid-metal artificial muscle architectures.
