Overview

Hydraulic and pneumatic actuators, as seminal technologies of the field of soft robotics (because of their high force output and large stroke), showcased the application of systems with inherent compliance towards augmenting traditional rigid robotics for enhanced collaboration. Despite their significant advantages, these systems were fundamentally limited by the need for external pumps making them bulky and ill-suited for easy integration and remote application. Within the broad landscape of emerging soft actuators, few platforms successfully combine low energy costs (e.g. low power input, high efficiency) with maximum performance (e.g. high energy-density, high speed). My thesis explored the unique potential of bioinspired fluid-based mechanisms where the medium acts as a transduction carrier – translating molecular scale chemical potential into macroscopic mechanical output – as a route towards improving the performance of photomechanical and electrochemical actuators.
